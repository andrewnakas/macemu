# The Residents' Bad Day on the Midway (Inscape, 1995) — Macintosh CD

**Hosted:** yes, as `bad-day-on-the-midway-68k-v1` (System 7.5.3 with the
application) beside `bad-day-on-the-midway-cd` (the disc's Macintosh volume).

## What this is

*Bad Day on the Midway*, written and directed by The Residents, animated and
designed by Jim Ludtke, developed by The Cryptic Corporation and published by
Inscape on 28 November 1995 on a hybrid Windows 3.1 and Macintosh CD-ROM. It
is a Macromedia Director projector ("BadDay") with its movies and media in a
folder on the disc.

## Rights

In March 2022 the copyright holder, The Cryptic Corporation, and Inscape's
former CEO Michael L. Nash allowed the game to be released as freeware. The
free package was put together by Max Wegner and is linked from The Residents'
own site (residents.com, "Historical" → Bad Day on the Midway). Its terms are
that it is free of charge, may not be sold, and that its makers are credited
with a link to https://www.wegneronline.com/badday/.

macemu charges nothing for it, credits that release and links to it on the
title page. The game and its contents remain © 1995 Cryptic Corporation,
Inscape and The Residents; nothing here claims otherwise.

If the rights holders ask, it goes the same day.

## Provenance

Internet Archive item `bad-day-on-the-midway`: an ISO of the original hybrid
disc with an Apple partition map. The Macintosh volume ("BadDay", 554 MB) is
served byte for byte as it sits in the disc's Apple_HFS partition.

The disc installs with a StuffIt InstallerMaker archive ("Bad Day Installer").
It was unpacked on the host, not run. Of its contents, only the 68k
application "BadDay (Non-Power PC)" was copied, unaltered and under its
original name, into System Folder:Startup Items on a System 7.5.3 disk, so the
system launches it at boot. It finds its media on the BadDay volume.

## System additions

The installer carries QuickTime and Sound Manager. The System 7.5.3 disk
already has Apple's QuickTime 2.5 and Sound Manager 3 installed (see
`notices/museum-of-anything-goes.md`), so none of the installer's own system
files were used.

## Why a Macintosh II

It runs the projector's 68k code on an emulated colour Macintosh II (Mini
vMac), the same route as The Museum of Anything Goes, which hung under Mac OS
8.6 on SheepShaver.

Played on 2026-10-05: the intro film, the opening ride, and the "She Knows
All" fortune teller with the thought captions running along the bottom.
