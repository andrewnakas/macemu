# The Museum of Anything Goes (Wayzata Technology, 1995) — Macintosh CD

**Hosted:** yes, as `museum-of-anything-goes-68k-v1` (System 7.5.3 with the
application) beside `museum-of-anything-goes-cd` (the disc's Macintosh volume).

## What this is

Version 1.0 of *The Museum of Anything Goes*, catalogue number 1411, made by
Michael Markowski and Maxwell S. Robinson and published in 1995 by Wayzata
Technology, Inc. of Grand Rapids, Minnesota, on a hybrid Windows 3.1 and
Macintosh CD-ROM. It is a Macromedia Director projector ("MacMoag") with its
media in folders beside it on the disc.

## Rights

Wayzata Technology closed in 1996, a year after this disc was published. The
title has been out of print since and is not part of any current product;
nobody sells it. That is a position about this specific out-of-commerce
artefact, not a claim that it is free of copyright. The authors' and any
successor's rights are acknowledged.

If the authors or a rights holder ask, it goes the same day.

## Provenance

Internet Archive item `museum-of-anything-goes` (uploaded 2015, in the CD-ROM
Software Library): an ISO of the hybrid disc with an Apple partition map. The
same disc is also preserved as `the-museum-of-anything-goes` and
`The_Museum_of_Anything_Goes_-_Wayzata_Technology_1411_1995`; their Apple_HFS
partitions are identical.

The Macintosh files were copied onto an HFS volume named "MOAG". That volume
carries every file from the disc's Macintosh partition, the Windows installer
and readme included, unaltered.

The application "MacMoag" was copied unaltered and under its original name
into System Folder:Startup Items on a System 7.5.3 disk, so the system launches
it at boot. It finds its media on the MOAG volume.

## System additions

The readme asks for Sound Manager 3.0 or better. The System 7.5.3 disk has
Apple's QuickTime 2.5 installed, which brings Sound Manager 3 with it. It was
installed by running Apple's own installer inside the emulated Mac (IA
`apple-computer-quicktime-2.5`; steps in `scripts/steps/qt25-install.json`).

## Why a Macintosh II

Under Mac OS 8.6 on an emulated PowerPC (SheepShaver) the projector stopped
at its first screen. On an emulated colour Macintosh II (Mini vMac) running its
68k code, it plays.

Played on 2026-10-04: the title screen, Skip Introduction, the courtyard, the
front doors and the first gallery corridor.
