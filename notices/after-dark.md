# After Dark 2.0 — provenance and licence

**Hosted:** yes, as `after-dark-v1`.
**Provenance:** abandoned commercial software.

## Where the files came from

The [`AfterDark_mac`](https://archive.org/details/AfterDark_mac) item at the
Internet Archive, which preserves a bootable System 7.5.3 disk with After Dark
2.0 already installed — the Archive serves it for in-browser emulation itself.
The volume was renamed and three pieces of unrelated cruft were removed
(`AppleShare PDS`, `TheVolumeSettingsFolder`, and a stray `Move&Rename`
folder); After Dark, its 71 modules and the System are untouched.

## Why it is here

This one is genuinely abandoned rather than freed, and the page says so.

Berkeley Systems was acquired in the late 1990s and dissolved into other
companies. The After Dark trademark has changed hands more than once since and
the Macintosh version has not been sold by anyone in roughly thirty years. No
rights holder distributes it, and no rights holder is identifiable without
guesswork.

It is preserved here on the same practical footing the Internet Archive and
Macintosh Garden operate on: software that is not commercially available and
not otherwise obtainable is worth keeping runnable. That is a position, not a
legal claim. If someone with standing objects, it goes — see `/takedown/`.

The reason it is worth the risk is that After Dark cannot be represented any
other way. It was a system extension, not an application: it hooks into the Mac
OS to take over an idle screen. The dozens of Flying Toasters tributes on the
web are re-creations in CSS and JavaScript. This is the software.

## Takedown

Anyone with standing to object can have this removed within 48 hours — see
